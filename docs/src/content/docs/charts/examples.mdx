The data on this page is made up, generated from a fixed seed. Switch the theme to see the charts follow it: they hold no hex values and no script.

## Three series

Direct labels on the lines, plus a legend. The first three slots are safe in any chart form.

<Chart chart={lineChart} alt="Line chart of three fictional series over 24 weeks. Cedar stays highest, Birch holds steady, Alder climbs towards it.">
  <Legend slot="before" names={SERIES} colors={categorical} />
</Chart>

## All eight slots

A stack only has neighbours touching, so it can use all eight.

<Chart chart={stackChart} alt="Stacked bars of eight fictional series across four quarters.">
  <Legend slot="before" names={STACK} colors={categorical} />
</Chart>

## One thing matters

Use one slot and the neutral tone when the story is about a few values. Bars keep close values easy to compare.

<RankedBars
  data={emphasisData}
  title="Fictional shares"
  alt="Ranked bars of seven fictional shares. Alder at 24% and Birch at 21% are highlighted. Cedar 16%, Dogwood 13%, Elm 10%, Fir 9%, Ginkgo 7%."
/>

## Parts of a whole

A donut, when a few parts and their total are the point. Every slice has its own slot, so you can match it to its label.

<Donut
  data={partsData}
  title={['Fictional', 'shares']}
  alt="Donut chart of five fictional shares. Alder 24%, Birch 21%, Cedar 16%, Dogwood 13%, Elm 10%."
/>

## How much

One hue. Step 1 means little and recedes into the surface, in both themes.

<Chart chart={heatmap} alt="Heatmap of a fictional value by hour and weekday, highest around midday on weekdays.">
  <Ramp colors={sequential} labels={['under 20', '20', '35', '50', '65', '80', '95+']} />
</Chart>
