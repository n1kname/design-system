## What it's for

A hierarchy whose point is its tiers: an organisation over its companies over their sites, and who uses all of it. Give it the tree and it lays it out: leaves left to right, each parent over the middle of what's under it, the lines down from each to its children.

## When not to use it

- **Anything that isn't a tree:** loops, people in several places, who pays whom. That's a [Flow diagram](/components/flow-diagram/), placed by hand.
- **Many leaves.** Past six or seven at the bottom it gets wide; a list or a table reads better.
- **Data.** Use a chart.

## Anatomy

- **Tier names:** down the left, in the mono face, one per row.
- **Nodes:** a label and an optional second line. The top tier is the deepest tint with a heavier edge, the next lighter, the rest plain.
- **Lines:** from each parent down to halfway, across, and down to each child.
- **Base band:** optional, `base`: a quiet band under the last tier, across all of it, with one line. Its name is the entry in `tiers` after the tree's own.
- **Caption:** optional.

## Variants

None. `root` takes one node or several.

## States

- **Arriving:** below the fold, it waits until it scrolls in, then builds top down: each tier settles in, then the lines draw down to the next. Already on screen, or under reduced motion, it is simply there.
- **Narrower than `minWidth`** (760 by default): it fits the width, smaller, and an **Enlarge** button under it opens it full size in a dialog, as [Flow diagram](/components/flow-diagram/) does.

## Keyboard and screen reader

- **It is one image** named by `title` and described by `desc`. Write `desc` as the hierarchy in sentences; the drawing itself isn't read out.
- **Enlarge** is a button named with the title; the dialog is modal and closes with Escape.

## Content

- **Labels:** the names as people use them.
- **Second lines:** what that tier does or holds, a few words joined with a middle dot.
