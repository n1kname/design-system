## What it's for

To say what a colour scale means, from low to high. It is made for the heat ramp on a map, and works for any ramp.

## When not to use it

- **Colours that name things,** such as kinds of route. Use a [legend](/components/legend/).
- **A scale whose values matter,** such as 0, 10 and 100. This says only low and high. Label the steps, or use a table.
- **A chart whose marks carry their own numbers.** It adds nothing.

## Anatomy

- **Title:** what the colour counts, in the text colour.
- **Note:** optional, after the title, such as how many cells there are.
- **Scale:** the low label, the ramp and the high label. The ramp is 120 by 10px, with a hairline edge so the yellow keeps its shape on a light surface.

## Variants

- **Smooth,** the default, for a scale that runs on: the heat ramp coloured by rank.
- **`steps`,** for a binned scale: each colour as a block.
- **`colors`,** for another ramp, low to high: the [sequential tokens](/charts/palette/#sequential), say.

## States

It has no states. Change the note from script through its `[data-ramp-note]` element when the data changes.

## Tokens

`--c-heat-1` to `--c-heat-6` by default, `--c-text`, `--c-text-secondary`, `--leading-caption`, `--sp-2`, `--text-caption`.

### The heat ramp

<div class="not-content">
  <div class="heat-swatches">
    {[1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6].map((i) => <div><i style={`background: var(--c-heat-${i})`} />heat-{i}</div>)}
  </div>
</div>

Cold to warm: blue, cyan, lime, yellow, orange and red. It is for a map's density. It is the same in both themes, because it sits on a basemap, not on the page. It lives in `@n1kname/tokens` with every other colour: `generate.mjs` writes it, and `check.mjs` asserts how far it holds.

- **Neighbours stay apart for colour-blind readers.** Lime and yellow are the closest, at 8.3 against a floor of 8.
- **Low isn't taken for high.** The ends are 22.9 apart for colour-blind readers.
- **A label inside any step reaches AA.** Steps 1 to 5 take the dark ink and step 6 takes white.
- **It reads by hue, not lightness.** Both ends sit near the same lightness, and the yellow in the middle is the lightest. In greyscale, low and high look alike. So pair it with a second cue: height in 3D, or this legend.
- **The yellow vanishes on a light surface,** at 1.3:1 on white. That's why the ramp has an edge here, and why it belongs on a basemap.

## Keyboard and screen reader

- **It reads as text:** the title and note, then "Low to High". The ramp itself is hidden.
- **Say the numbers elsewhere.** A ramp gives the shape. The hover card, or a table, gives each count.

## Content

- **Title it by what is counted,** such as "Visits" or "GPS points", not "Density".
- **Keep the ends to one word,** such as Low and High, or Few and Many.

## From script

A canvas map reads colours, not tokens. `ramp()` in `mapstage.js` turns the heat tokens, or any list of colours, into a MapLibre expression with the colours resolved. It colours by a value from 0 to 1. Build it again when the theme changes.

```js
import { onTheme, ramp } from '@n1kname/components/mapstage.js';

map.addLayer({ id: 'cells', type: 'fill-extrusion', source: 'cells', paint: { 'fill-extrusion-color': ramp('rank') } });
onTheme(() => map.setPaintProperty('cells', 'fill-extrusion-color', ramp('rank')));
document.querySelector('#legend [data-ramp-note]').textContent = ` · ${cells.length} cells`;
```
