The best motion goes unnoticed. It's there to give feedback or to show where something came from, never to decorate.

## Tokens

| Token | Value | For |
|---|---|---|
| `--motion-fast` | 120ms | Hover and press |
| `--motion-base` | 180ms | Small things appearing, such as a chart settling in |
| `--motion-slow` | 260ms | A panel, the largest thing that moves |
| `--motion-gentle` | 240ms | Hover on a portfolio |
| `--motion-reveal` | 900ms | The one opening of a portfolio page |
| `--ease-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)` | Things arriving: fast start, soft landing |
| `--ease-exit` | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1)` | Things leaving for good |
| `--ease-standard` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0.38, 0.9)` | Things leaving that stay within reach, such as a side panel |
| `--ease-soft` | `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` | Long reveals: most of the travel comes early, so it answers at once |
| `--motion-spring`, `--ease-spring` | 340ms, a sampled spring | Something large arriving that should settle rather than stop, such as a side panel |
| `--motion-hover`, `--ease-hover` | Fast and out, by default | What every hover uses. A site overrides these two, not each component |

## Spring

`--ease-spring` is a spring (stiffness 380, damping 24, mass 0.6) sampled into CSS `linear()`, so it needs no script. It covers most of the distance early, goes about 1.6% past the end and settles back, over `--motion-spring`. On a 24px slide that overshoot is under half a pixel: you feel it more than see it.

Use it for arriving only, and on `transform`. Leaving stays on `--ease-standard` or `--ease-exit`, and opacity stays on `--ease-out`, so the fade is done before the settle. The side panel opens on it.

The numbers live in `generate.mjs`. Change the stiffness, damping or mass there, and the curve and its duration follow.

## Charts arriving

A chart arrives once, the first time it comes into view, so the eye follows how it is built:

- **Bars** grow from the zero line on the spring, left to right, all of them within 420ms. Bars below zero grow down from it, and a stack grows as one bar.
- **Lines** draw from their first point over a second. Their dots appear as the line reaches them. A dashed line fades in instead, because its dash is its own.
- **Heatmap cells** fill in column by column.
- **Words and shading**, such as value labels and line names, land last.
- **Ranked bars** grow from the left, top to bottom.

The chart builders tag their marks (`enter-bar`, `enter-line`, `enter-dot`, `enter-cell`, `enter-fade`, `enter-zero`), and one browser helper plays them. It uses the Web Animations API, so it needs no stylesheet and leaves the marks' own transforms alone.

```js
import { enter, enterAll } from '@n1kname/charts/enter';

enter(svg);              // a chart drawn in the page: its first drawing only
enterAll('.chart-panel'); // charts drawn at build time
```

Nothing is hidden until the helper runs, so without script a chart is simply there. Under reduced motion it does nothing. A drawing that isn't shown, such as the narrow one of a pair, is left alone.

## Charts changing

When a chart is drawn again with new figures, such as another year from a [Period picker](/components/period-picker/), it changes in place instead of arriving again, so the eye follows what grew and what shrank:

- **A bar that was there before** moves from its old place and size to its new ones, on the spring.
- **A bar with no match** grows from the zero line, as on arrival.
- **The axis words and grid** fade in over `--motion-base`.
- **A figure beside the chart,** such as a stat, settles in: it fades up from a little below. It never counts up or down.

A bar is matched by its series and by a key you give for its place on the x axis. Key months by their month of the year when stepping through years, so January moves to January; key them by the month itself when the months shown change, so different months are never shown turning into each other.

```js
import { snapshot, update, settle } from '@n1kname/charts/update';

const key = year === 'all' ? String : (m) => m.slice(5, 7); // '2025-01' → '01'
const before = snapshot(box.querySelector('svg'), oldKey);
box.replaceChildren(svg);
update(before, svg, key);
settle(statElement);
```

Never on a resize, and nothing under reduced motion. The tokens are read with their unit, so `.34s` is 340ms.

## Under the pointer

The card, rule and dot of a chart's [pointer](/charts/pointer/) glide from step to step on the spring (`--motion-spring`, `--ease-spring`), so the eye follows where it went. The figures in the card change at once. It appears over `--motion-fast` and leaves the same way, a moment after the pointer does, so crossing a gap doesn't make it blink. The bars or slices not pointed at dim over `--motion-base`. Nothing moves under reduced motion.

## Changing page and theme

Both are one cross-fade of the whole page, through view transitions, so nothing else on the page has to animate.

- **Page change:** `page-change.css` cross-fades over `--motion-base`, and [Page nav](/components/page-nav/)'s pill, or [Side nav](/components/side-nav/)'s marker, glides to the new page's link on the spring. Your eye follows it, so you know where you landed. A side nav's column holds still, and its words lie above the marker, so nothing in it flickers. Page nav's pill glides as an outline, so the links it passes stay readable, and fills in as it lands. Charts drawn in the page wait for the glide to finish before they draw.
- **Theme change:** [Theme toggle](/components/theme-toggle/) eases the page into its new colours over `--motion-base`.
- **A picture that carries over:** a [Link card](/components/link-card/)'s picture grows into the picture with the same name on the page it opens, on the spring. For a portfolio index and its cases, not for data pages.

A browser without view transitions changes at once, and so does everyone who asks for reduced motion.

## Two modes

Carbon calls these productive and expressive, and that split works here too.

| | Data pages | Portfolio |
|---|---|---|
| **Job** | Get out of the way | Set a mood, once |
| **Hover** | `fast`, `out` | `gentle`, `soft` |
| **Opening** | Charts arrive once, about a second, and the answer's highlight sweeps in | `reveal`, `soft`, about 70ms apart |
| **Page change** | A 180ms cross-fade, the nav pill or marker glides | A 180ms cross-fade |
| **New figures** | Bars move to their new heights, stats settle | None |
| **Ambient** | None | Colour and grain on case heroes only, still |

A portfolio sets these once, and every component follows:

```css
:root {
  --motion-hover: var(--motion-gentle);
  --ease-hover: var(--ease-soft);
}
```

## Rules

- **Ask how often it happens.** Something you trigger all day should never make you wait. The chart [pointer](/charts/pointer/) glides from step to step, but its figures change at once: only where it is moves, never what it says.
- **Smaller things move faster.** A button is `fast`, a panel is `slow`.
- **Leaving is quicker than arriving,** and travels less. Your attention has already moved on.
- **One moment, then stop.** Charts arrive once, as they come into view, and not on every resize or redraw.
- **Move `transform` and `opacity` only.** Animating width or height makes the whole page work harder. The one exception is [App shell](/components/app-shell/)'s column folding, which moves only when you ask it to and frees room that has to be laid out anyway.
- **No looping motion to get attention.** No pulsing dots, no breathing buttons.

## Reduced motion

`tokens.css` cuts every animation and transition to almost nothing when someone asks their system for less motion. Anything that also moves, such as a press scale, turns that off in its own `prefers-reduced-motion` block, so the state still changes without the travel.
